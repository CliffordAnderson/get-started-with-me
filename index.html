Get started with

Short lessons in machine learning, each built around one idea small enough to hold in your hand and operate.

1
perceptrons

What a single artificial neuron computes, why its answer is always a straight line drawn through the space of its inputs, and how it finds that line from labelled examples by correcting its own mistakes.

Four panels, about ten minutes. You place the examples and run the learning rule on them.
2
cats and dogs

The same unit as the first lesson, handed 144 inputs and a pile of small pictures. What its weights look like when you fold them back into an image, why that image is the whole of what it knows, and the two-pixel nudge that takes it apart.

Five panels, about twelve minutes. You train it, read its template, then break it.
3
exclusive or

The smallest function a single perceptron cannot compute, the argument that proves it, and what happens to the space when you put a second layer in the way.

Seven panels, about fifteen minutes. You try to draw the line yourself before finding out why it is impossible.
4
backpropagation

Where the weights in the last lesson came from. A smooth unit has a slope you can walk down, and the chain rule shares the blame backwards through a hidden layer nobody ever told what to say — along with the guarantee that is lost on the way.

Five panels, about fifteen minutes. You walk the error surface by hand, then let two hundred restarts settle how often it works.

What these are

Explanations you can take hold of. Every diagram on every page is computed in your browser from the numbers currently on screen: move a slider and the mathematics runs again. Nothing is a recording, so when a demonstration fails it is failing for the real reason, and you can go looking for it.

How to read one

Each lesson is a few short panels with a rail across the top. They are written in order and build an argument, but any panel will stand on its own if you would rather jump. Nothing to install, no account, and every page works from a phone.

Technical words are linked to the glossary where they first appear, and every entry there explains the term plainly before pointing at Wikipedia for the long version.

Written and built by hand. The lessons are independent of one another except where one says otherwise.
